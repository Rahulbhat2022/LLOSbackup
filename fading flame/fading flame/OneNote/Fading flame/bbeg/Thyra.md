# Thyra — The Ashwright

### _"My husband wanted to burn the throne. I want to bury it so deep nothing grows here again."_

---

## Overview

Thyra is not the villain this story started with. She is the one it made.

Ivor, the original Ashwright, was a man of grief and ideology — a revolutionary who lost his daughter on a failed expedition, who traded his health and sanity piece by piece to build a weapon that would end the Highborn's stranglehold on Solace. He had a plan. He had principles, however corroded. He believed that in the city's ashes, something better could be built.

Ivor died before he finished. The radiation sickness took him slowly — coughing blood in a basement in the Scourge, hallucinating his dead children, fading like a coal going dark. He left behind notes, contacts, half-finished schematics, and a wife who had already lost everything else.

Thyra picked up the mantle not out of ideology. Out of having nothing left to protect.

Where Ivor was a burning man, Thyra is something colder. He wanted justice. She has stopped believing justice is a thing that exists. She has one remaining purpose: the Highborn districts will not survive her. The rest she has stopped thinking about.

---

## Who She Was

Thyra was not a revolutionary before the losses. She was a Markfolk-born physician — self-trained, brilliant, working out of the Ledge treating people the Committee's medical system had abandoned. She met Ivor through his early activist work, when he was still trying to change things through legitimate channels. She believed in him then. She believed in the cause.

She had two children with him. Both are dead. The first — a daughter, Mira — was lost on the same failed expedition that broke Ivor and set him on his final path. The second — a son, Calder — died during one of Ivor's early ether test detonations, in an accident Ivor never fully forgave himself for.

And then Ivor died.

Thyra buried him in the Scourge with no marker. Then she went to his workshop, sat down with his notes, and started reading.

---

## What She Became

The title of Ashwright is not inherited — it was written into Ivor's notes as a role, not a name. _The one who shapes the ash. The architect of the end._ He wrote it as a reminder to himself of what he was doing and why. Thyra read it as instructions.

She is methodical in a way Ivor never was. He gathered components desperately, driven by deteriorating health and increasing paranoia. Thyra approached the same task like a physician diagnosing a terminal patient: systematically, without sentiment, cataloguing what remained and what still needed to be acquired.

She is also more dangerous because she has no interest in surviving the outcome.

Ivor wanted to watch Solace rebuilt from the Highborn's ruins. Thyra has no such fantasy. She intends to destroy the High Courts, the Citadel, the Sanctum — and she intends to be somewhere nearby when it happens. Whether she survives is not a variable she is optimizing for.

---

## The Outside Source — The Advanced City

What Thyra has that Ivor never did is a completed blueprint.

Approximately eight months after Ivor's death, Thyra was contacted through an intermediary — a courier she never met face to face, speaking in written messages that burned after being read. The messages offered technical knowledge: how to stabilize the ether core, how to direct the detonation upward through the Citadel's foundations, how to time the ignition sequence to ensure the device couldn't be disarmed once triggered.

She did not ask who was sending the information. She understood enough to know the offer wasn't altruistic — someone wanted Solace destroyed and was using her to do it. She accepted the knowledge anyway. Her goal and theirs happened to align. She considers this irrelevant.

What she does not know — what the Advanced City's operatives carefully omitted — is that the device they've helped her build will not merely destroy the High Courts. The ether-amplified detonation channeled through the Eternal Flame vein beneath the Citadel will produce a radiation cascade across the entire city. The Scourge, the Ledge, the Strand, Veil's End — everyone. The Ashenfolk she has spent her adult life treating. The Heartfolk children at the parade. Everyone.

The Advanced City wants a controlled demolition of Solace's population so they can move into the power vacuum and access the ether deposits beneath the city. They have told Thyra she is saving the poor. They have told her she is a surgeon cutting out a tumor. They have given her just enough truth to make the lie invisible.

## Her Plan — Stages

### Stage I — Acquisition (Complete)

The components Ivor gathered before his death were sufficient for a prototype — not a functional weapon. Thyra has spent the intervening months completing the acquisition of:

- **Refined Ether:** Secured through a corrupt contact at a Strand refinery, using funds from Ivor's remaining network.
- **Soulgold Casing:** Obtained via the black market — she did not acquire it from the Takahashis directly, but through a disgraced engineer who had stolen partial specifications. The casing is functional but imperfect, which she knows. She has calculated the variance into the detonation timing.
- **Conduit Soul:** The most difficult component. She has identified a candidate — a magically potent individual whose death would finalize the device's ignition sequence. She has not yet acted on this.

### Stage II — Infrastructure (In Progress)

The Advanced City's blueprints require the device to be placed at a specific point beneath the Citadel — an access tunnel connected to the Eternal Flame vein, used by maintenance workers and long since forgotten by anyone above the Ledge. Thyra has been mapping these tunnels for three months, sometimes herself, sometimes through hired contractors who believe they are doing survey work for a new water line.

She has also been quietly placing secondary charges — smaller ether caches along the Citadel's structural supports, enough to collapse the outer walls inward. These are a redundancy measure. If the primary device fails, the secondary charges will ensure the Citadel comes down.

### Stage III — The Conduit (Pending)

The device requires a living conduit — someone whose magical essence is used to trigger the final ignition. Thyra's notes describe this as "the closing of the circuit." The conduit does not survive the process.

She has a candidate in mind. She has not moved on this yet because she is not ready. The candidate's death will be the point of no return, and Thyra, despite everything, is not someone who acts carelessly. She will move when all other components are in place.

### Stage IV — Detonation

The plan calls for the device to be triggered during a high-attendance Citadel event — ideally one that draws the major Highborn houses, the Church of the Eternal Light, and the Kemura into the building simultaneously. Her preference is a Debt Hearing session or a formal Citadel convening.

She intends to trigger it herself.

## Why She Cannot Be Talked Down — DM Notes

This section is the most important for running her correctly. The party may reach her. They may learn the truth — that the radiation will kill the entire city, not just the Highborn. They may try to tell her. She will not be moved. Here is why, and how to play it:

### She Already Knows About Collateral Damage

Not the full truth — she doesn't know about the radiation cascade. But she has always known that a bomb beneath the Citadel would kill people beyond the High Courts. She made peace with that early. She told herself the number was manageable. She told herself the lives of Highborn servants and Citadel staff were the price of stopping a system that was already killing people every day — just slowly, quietly, with paperwork.

This means when the party tells her people will die, her first response is not shock. It is: _I know. I decided that was acceptable._ The party has to then escalate — the radiation won't kill hundreds, it will kill everyone. Every Ashenfolk. Every Hearthfolk. The Scourge will die last and slowest.

### She Has No Attachment to Outcomes

Ivor wanted to rebuild. Thyra doesn't. She is not trying to create a better Solace — she is trying to end this one. If the party argues "but what about the people who will survive and build something better," she has no purchase on that idea. She doesn't believe a better city comes after. She has stopped believing in afters.

She is not suicidal in the clinical sense — she is _done._ There is a difference. She isn't seeking death. She just doesn't require survival as a condition for success.

**How to play this:** When the party makes utilitarian arguments ("more people will be saved if you don't do this"), she engages coldly and rationally. She is not ranting. She is not crying. She walks them through her logic the way a doctor explains a terminal prognosis. The horror is that she is _reasonable_. Her conclusions are catastrophic but her reasoning is internally consistent.

### She Has Been Lied To — But It Doesn't Matter Anymore

If the party somehow proves to her that the Advanced City manipulated her — that the radiation cascade is real, that she was used — she will believe them. She is a physician. She understands evidence.

And then she will continue anyway.

This is the most important beat for the DM to play correctly. She will say something like: _"They lied to me about the scope. That changes who benefits from this. It doesn't change what this city is."_

She has crossed a threshold where her reasoning has become self-sustaining. The Advanced City gave her the blueprint. But the decision to use it was always hers. Removing the outside manipulation doesn't undo the internal logic she has built over years of grief and loss. She is not a puppet who falls apart when the strings are cut.

### She Loves Her Child

One of the party members is her child. (See below.) This is the only real point of vulnerability — not a weakness that stops her plan, but a wound that bleeds through her composure.

She is not estranged from this child out of coldness. She removed herself from their life because she decided the plan was more important than they were, and she knew that if she stayed close, she might hesitate. She was right. She does hesitate, in her way, when confronted. But she has mistaken the absence of her child's presence for the absence of attachment, and she is wrong about that.

**How to play this:** She doesn't plead with them. She doesn't try to recruit them. She tells them the truth — that she loves them, that she made her choices knowing the cost, and that she is asking them, once, to stand aside. She won't harm them if she can avoid it. She will not stop.

The tragedy of this confrontation is that she is not wrong that the city is broken. She is not wrong that the Highborn have been destroying lives for centuries. She is wrong that this is the answer, and she knows it, somewhere deep down, and has buried it under years of grief so thoroughly that the party finding it and pulling it back out may be the only thing that can reach her.

Or it may not be enough.

## Physical Description and Mannerisms

- Mid-50s. Lean in the way people get when they've stopped eating regularly. Hair gone fully white, worn pulled back severely.
- Hands are a physician's hands — steady, practiced, slightly calloused. She still treats patients occasionally in the Scourge. She has not stopped thinking of herself as a healer.
- Speaks quietly and precisely. Does not raise her voice. When she is most upset, she becomes more measured, not less — the opposite of most people.
- Carries a small leather case that contains both medical instruments and ether testing equipment. She has never separated the two.
- Smells like antiseptic and burnt metal.
- Has a habit of going still when she's thinking — not frozen, just very, very quiet. It unsettles people.

---

## Running Her in Scenes

**Before the party knows who she is:** She can appear as a figure in the background — an old woman in the Scourge who treats the sick, who seems to know more than she says, who watches the party with a specific quality of attention that isn't threatening but isn't quite neutral either. If Siobhan is with the party, there are moments of strange recognition that neither can quite name.

**When the party begins closing in on the Ashwright:** She becomes aware of them earlier than they might expect — she has been watching the city's moving pieces for two years. She does not try to kill them. She does try to redirect them: sending them on paths that take them away from her operation, occasionally giving them information that helps them deal with threats that might expose her.

**The confrontation:** She chooses the location. She has had time to prepare. She is not in a panic. She will speak to them first. She will not waste the time she has, because she knows this conversation may be the last one that matters.

---

## Stat Block Notes (Final Boss — Act 1)

Thyra fights like a physician who decided to become a weapon.

- **Primary:** Ether-based alchemical grenades — she carries six, each calibrated differently. Concussive, incendiary, blinding, and one that produces a localized radiation field.
- **Secondary:** She is not a martial combatant. She is extremely hard to pin down — she moves constantly, uses the environment, positions the party so they are between her and the collateral.
- **Special mechanic — The Device:** If the device has been placed and primed by the time of the fight, there is a countdown mechanic running in the background. She is not trying to kill the party. She is trying to survive long enough for the timer to run out. This reframes the fight entirely — she doesn't need to win. She needs to _stall._
- **If reduced to 0 HP:** She does not die immediately in the conventional sense. She has a contingency — a dead-man's trigger on her person that will detonate one charge if she goes down. The party has a choice: kill her and deal with the trigger, or find another way.

## Faction Relationships

**The Advanced City:** Her handlers. She knows they are using her and doesn't care. They have given her everything she asked for and she has no illusions about their motives. She simply intends to use their blueprint before they can redirect her.

**The Embers:** She knows of them. Aristide Voss and Arvid Voss are pursuing similar ends through different means. She does not coordinate with them — she considers their approach too slow and too concerned with surviving the aftermath. They are not aware of her plan's full scope.

**The Kemura:** She has been careful to avoid their notice. The Kemura's interest in magical order would make her operation a direct target if discovered. Her ether work is deliberately conducted below the threshold of arcane signatures they monitor.

**Ivor's Remaining Network:** She inherited his contacts — the corrupt refinery foreman, the Iron Clutch fence who moved his materials, the former Kemura discard who helped with the soulgold casing. Some of these people know they are still working for the Ashwright. None of them know Thyra is who she is. To them, she is a handler passing on instructions from a figure they've never met.

---

## The Question the Party Has to Answer

Thyra is not wrong that Solace is broken. The caste system, the Committee, the Highborn's excess, the slow death of the Ashenfolk — she has watched all of it her entire life and she is not mistaken in her diagnosis.

She is catastrophically wrong about the treatment.

The party's job, in the end, is not to convince her she's wrong about the disease. It's to stop her from burning everyone alive with the cure.

Whether they do that by stopping the device, by reaching her through Siobhan, by exposing the Advanced City's manipulation, or by finding a third option they create themselves — that is the story.

The worst outcome is that they stop her too late. The second worst is that she dies having never understood what she was being used for.

The best outcome is that she lives, and has to reckon with what she was going to do, and what it means that she was going to do it. That is not a comfortable ending. That is an honest one.
