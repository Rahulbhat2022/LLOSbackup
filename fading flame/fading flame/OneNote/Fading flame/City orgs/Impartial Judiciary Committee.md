## Overview

The Impartial Judiciary Committee — known colloquially as **the Scales**, **the Blind Court**, or more bitterly among the lower castes as **the Bought Bench** — is the closest thing Solace has to a city-wide judicial institution. It was established generations ago as a neutral arbitration body, meant to resolve disputes that crossed district lines and fell outside the jurisdiction of any single governing faction.

In theory, the Committee is the one institution in Solace that bows to no lord, no guild, no church. In practice, it is an elegant machine for legitimizing whatever outcome the powerful have already decided.

---

## Structure

The Committee is divided into three chambers, each theoretically balancing the others.

### The Upper Chamber — The Weighers

The Weighers are the seven senior arbiters who issue final rulings in high-stakes cases: land and property disputes between noble houses, criminal charges leveled at individuals above a certain caste standing, and any matter involving cross-district commerce above a certain gold threshold. Appointments to the Upper Chamber are for life, confirmed by a majority of Citadel seats.

Because the Citadel is dominated by the Highborn houses, the Weighers have historically reflected the interests of those who appointed them. There is no requirement that a Weigher be educated in law — only that they be "of sound moral character and recognized standing." In practice, this means Wall Born or above.

Current notable Weighers include:

- **Arbiter Sorin Vael** — an elderly former merchant lord, widely believed to be in the pocket of House Valeheart and their Aetherium interests.
- **Arbiter Maren Cass** — the only Markfolk-born Weigher in living memory, a former city scribe who clawed her way up through decades of legal work. She is incorruptible in the way only someone who has lost everything once can be. House Valcort quietly supports her continued seat.

### The Middle Chamber — The Readers

The Readers are the working body of the Committee: roughly forty clerks, lawkeepers, and trained jurists who handle the vast majority of cases — mostly civil disputes, debt proceedings, and minor criminal matters among the Wall Born and Markfolk. They are genuinely skilled, often idealistic when they enter the institution, and ground down within a decade.

The Readers cannot issue rulings; they can only prepare cases, gather testimony, and present findings upward. This means a Reader can spend three years building an airtight case against a Highborn landlord for burning down a tenement full of Ashenfolk — and then watch a Weigher dismiss it in an afternoon with two sentences and no explanation.

The Readers know what the institution is. Most of them stay anyway. Some because they believe in the rare cases where justice actually happens. Some because the salary is the best available work for their caste. A few because they are quietly building evidence for something larger.

### The Lower Chamber — The Petitioners' Desk

The only point of contact the Gutterborn and Ashenfolk have with the Committee. The Petitioners' Desk technically accepts cases from any citizen of Solace regardless of stamp, but cases from the lower castes are subject to a **filing bond** — a deposit of coin, held against "frivolous use of Committee resources." The bond for Markfolk is manageable. For Ashenfolk, it is three months' wages. For Gutterborn, it effectively does not exist as an option.

Cases that survive the bond are assigned to a **Petitions Clerk**, who is almost always overworked, underfunded, and handling forty other cases. The average processing time for a lower-caste petition is fourteen months. Most petitioners give up, die, or relocate before seeing a resolution.

## The Seal and Symbolism

The Committee's official seal is two hands holding a scale in balance, with a blindfold across the wrists — the implication being that justice is administered without sight of who holds the petition. Satirists in the Scourge have modified this image for years: the hands wearing aristocratic rings, the scales tipped to one side, the blindfold placed deliberately over the eyes of the petitioner, not the arbiter.

The Committee has attempted to have these images suppressed. The Jesters distribute new versions faster than they can be torn down.

## Jurisdiction and Reach

The Committee holds formal jurisdiction over cross-district disputes and any matter involving parties from two or more governing regions. In practice:

- **The High Courts / Sanctum**: Nobles rarely submit to Committee authority. They have their own arbitration processes and only bring cases to the Scales when it benefits them — usually to use the Committee's official legitimacy to crush a rival.
- **The Ledge and the Heart**: The most active users of the Committee, particularly for commercial disputes. The Wardens defer to Committee rulings in theory, though enforcement is contracted through the Iron Clutch, which introduces its own complications.
- **The Strand**: Factory and industry disputes are technically under Committee jurisdiction, but the Strand's legislation is so weighted toward ownership that most labor complaints are dismissed at intake.
- **Veil's End**: The Committee has no functional reach here. Attempting to enforce a ruling in Veil's End requires hiring your own muscle, at which point you are essentially conducting a private war.
- **The Scourge**: Committee rulings exist on paper. Enforcement does not.

## Notable Functions and Events

### The Debt Hearings

Twice per year, the Committee holds open sessions for debt disputes — the only time the chambers are genuinely open to the public. These are theatrical events: creditors present claims, debtors defend themselves, and crowds pack the gallery. The Iron Clutch maintains a significant presence at Debt Hearings, as a substantial portion of Solace's outstanding debt passes through their ledgers in some form. Master Glint the Loanshark has been known to attend personally, seated in the public gallery with a smile that never quite reaches his eyes.

### The Charter Review

Every five years, the Committee is supposed to review its own charter, updating procedures and re-ratifying its independence. The last Charter Review was nine years ago. The next has been "postponed pending resource allocation" for four years running. Arbiter Vael has not explained why.

### Sealed Judgments

A peculiarity of Committee procedure: rulings involving parties above a certain caste or wealth threshold can be issued as **Sealed Judgments** — binding and enforceable, but with the details of the ruling hidden from public record. The stated reason is protecting sensitive commercial information. Critics note that Sealed Judgments have never once gone against a Highborn petitioner.

## Relationship with Other Factions

**House Valcort / Anastasia Valcort**: Anastasia has appeared before the Committee multiple times challenging exploitative laws and landlord practices. She has won more cases than anyone expected and lost more than she should have. She has a complicated respect for Arbiter Maren Cass and a quietly documented contempt for Arbiter Vael.

**The Embers**: The revolutionist movement views the Committee as a prop — a piece of furniture arranged to make the room look legitimate. They don't waste energy attacking it directly. They do, however, circulate records of dismissed Ashenfolk petitions as recruitment material.

**The Iron Clutch**: The Committee uses Iron Clutch-contracted enforcers to carry out rulings in the lower districts. This means the Iron Clutch knows, in advance, what the Committee is going to order enforced — and can choose how vigorously to pursue it. The Clutch has, on occasion, "lost" enforcement papers for rulings that would hurt their interests.

**The Jesters**: Keep meticulous, secret records of every Committee ruling. No one is sure why. The leading theory is that they are waiting for the right moment to release all of it at once.

**The Church of the Eternal Light**: Holds a formal "advisory relationship" with the Committee, which in practice means the Church sends a representative to Upper Chamber deliberations involving moral or spiritual questions. The Church considers this arrangement extremely useful. The Committee considers it an embarrassing necessity.

## Adventure Hooks
Quiet justice
- A Reader named **Edwyn Solt** has been quietly documenting every Sealed Judgment for twelve years, building a record he plans to leak. He has recently been told he is being transferred to a record-keeping post in the Scourge — effectively a death sentence for his career, possibly literally. He needs someone to get his documents out before the transfer.
Pay the bribe
- An Ashenfolk woman named **Brix** paid her entire family's savings to file a petition against a Strand factory owner who collapsed a work tunnel, killing her brother. The case has been in the Petitioners' Desk for sixteen months. She's run out of money to bribe her way to a faster hearing. She is starting to listen to the Embers' arguments about what real justice looks like.
