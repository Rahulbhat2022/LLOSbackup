# The Chimney Ward

**"A full stomach asks fewer questions."**

---

## Overview

The Chimney Ward smells better than anywhere else in the Hearth, which is its most effective deception. The district produces the food economy of the middle city: its guild-run bakeries and communal kitchens, its licensed tavern-kitchens and ingredient markets, its preserved goods storehouses and the regulated meal services that keep the Hearth's workforce fed on a schedule. Walking through the Ward at midday, with bread smell and spice and rendered fat drifting through the avenues, it is easy to feel that things are basically fine.

The Ward's communal kitchens — large, guild-run facilities that serve hot meals to registered Hearth residents at a set daily rate — are the most important infrastructure in the district. They are also the most political. Who can access them, at what price, and on whose terms is a question that the Ward's head has managed to keep extremely complicated for a very long time.

The ingredient market runs in the centre of the Ward each morning: wholesale quantities of produce, grain, preserved goods, and imported luxuries. The prices are public, posted on large chalk boards. The prices are also adjusted twice daily, and the chalk board update lags by forty minutes for non-guild buyers. In those forty minutes, the guild intermediaries lock in prices that the unlicensed vendors pay more for tomorrow.

## Oswin Char — Head of the Chimney Ward
![[Pasted image 20260722135501.png]]

Orion Char is an enormously cheerful man and it is very difficult to stay angry at him for long, which he has always considered his most useful attribute. He is heavyset, red-faced, and loud in the way that makes people feel included rather than overwhelmed. He shakes hands with both of his. He laughs at his own jokes a half-second before the punchline, which should be annoying and somehow isn't. He knows everyone in the Ward by name, including the names of their dogs.

He runs the **Chimney Guild** — which controls communal kitchen licensing, communal meal pricing, and ingredient market access fees — with an absolute grip wrapped in a jovial exterior. The communal kitchens are nominally subsidised services; in practice, their pricing is calibrated precisely to sit just within what the median Marktfolk worker can afford on a standard working week, with no margin remaining for savings. This is not an accident. Oswin has the data, reviewed quarterly, and adjusts accordingly. He sells *just enough* comfort that people don't go hungry, but not enough that they accrue anything that looks like security.

He has a secondary arrangement with the Gilded Tongue — moneylending access to families who fall behind on kitchen fees. The referral fee he takes per family converted to a debtor is modest and, in aggregate, represents his most reliable income stream. He thinks of it as keeping people connected to the district's resources.

He would give you his coat off his back and also pick your pocket while doing it, and you would feel warm all the way home.

**Voice:** Loud and warm, with a laughing quality at the end of sentences. He calls people "friend" immediately. He is not faking the warmth. He has simply integrated it into the extraction.

## Notable Locations

- **The Grand Communal** — The largest kitchen facility in the Ward, capable of serving several hundred meals per sitting. Clean, warm, efficiently run. The queue forms an hour before service. Registered Marktfolk pay the daily rate. Hearthfolk pay slightly more for the same access.
- **The Morning Market** — The ingredient exchange, running dawn to mid-morning. The price boards are accurate, in sequence, and forty minutes behind.
- **Char's Table** — Oswin's personal tavern-kitchen, which serves both as his office and the most pleasant meal in the Hearth. He eats with his workers every afternoon. He picks up the bill. He writes it off against guild dues.
- **The Cold Store** — A warded preservation facility beneath the Ward where surplus is kept. Access is licensed. The volume of actual surplus is a closely held figure. There is always surplus; it is priced at a premium during shortages that Oswin declares unilaterally.
