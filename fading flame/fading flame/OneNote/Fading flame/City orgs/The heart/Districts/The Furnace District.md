# The Furnace District

**"The cold doesn't care who you voted for."**

---

## Overview

The Furnace District is the oldest slice of the Hearth and the one that considers itself most essential — with some justification. The district is built above and around the city's central heating infrastructure: a network of massive furnaces, pressure boilers, and heat-pipes that run beneath the Hearth's streets and feed warmth to hundreds of buildings. Without the Furnace District, the Hearth goes cold. Without the Hearth going cold, the Marktfolk don't quite understand who holds the real leverage here.

On the surface level the district is a dense corridor of forge-shops, smithies, metalworks, and coal yards. The air is always ten degrees warmer than neighbouring districts. Soot settles on everything. Workers' faces are permanently flushed. The forges run on day and night shifts, their orange glow visible from the Plaza of Ambition at any hour. It smells of burning metal, hot coal, and the occasional scorched leather of someone who stood too close.

The district's residents are proud people — Marktfolk who believe in the dignity of physical work, who wear their burn scars like medals, who would sooner eat cold food than admit they needed a favour. The Furnace District has the highest rate of guild membership in the Hearth, the highest rate of workplace accidents, and the lowest rate of complaint filings. These three facts are related.

---

## Marta Ashveil — Head of the Furnace District

Marta Ashveil came up through the forge floors the same way most people do: slowly, painfully, and by making herself indispensable. She was a common smith at seventeen, a foreman at twenty-four, and by thirty she had consolidated three competing smithing guilds into the **Ashveil Union** through a combination of genuine respect and perfectly timed debt collection on her rivals. She is now fifty-one, and her grip on the Furnace District is absolute.

She is a broad, scarred woman with iron-grey hair kept short and a left hand that doesn't fully close — legacy of a forge accident she refuses to discuss. She runs Craft Council meetings with blunt efficiency and speaks in clipped sentences. She does not threaten. She simply reminds people what happens when pipes run cold.

What the Furnace District's workers do not know — and what Marta has maintained carefully for fifteen years — is that the heat distributed through the district pipes passes through a series of *tap valves* that she controls personally. A portion of the heat generated is siphoned before it reaches the poorest residential sections of the Hearth and sold back to those same residents at winter rates through licensed "warming stations." She calls this a supplemental service. Her accountant calls it her largest revenue stream. The workers who operate the tap valves call her *the Damper*, quietly, when she isn't listening.

**Voice:** Flat. Direct. The kind of calm that only people who have real power are ever fully comfortable with. She doesn't raise her voice because she has never needed to.

## Notable Locations

- **The Main Furnace Hall** — A cathedral-like industrial space housing the six primary boilers. Access is restricted to licensed Union workers. The heat inside is physically oppressive. Rumour holds that the lower levels connect to Ring tunnels.
- **The Ashveil Forge** — Marta's personal smithy, now mostly ceremonial. She still works a few hours a week to keep her hands in and her mystique intact.
- **The Coal Exchange** — Where bulk fuel contracts are bought, sold, and renewed. Every major supplier in Solace passes through here. Marta has her fingers in most of the terms.
- **Warming Station Seven** — The largest of the district's paid warming stations, located precisely where the residential heat is most throttled. Always has a queue of cold people with coins.

## Quest Hook — The Cold Shoulder

In the middle of a particularly brutal winter, Warming Station Seven charges double. Marta claims the main boilers had a "pressure failure." Three Union workers know she shut the valves herself and are looking for someone to bring their evidence to — someone who isn't already in her pocket. The party must decide: expose her and destabilise the district's most critical infrastructure, or find a way to leverage the information for something more useful than justice.
