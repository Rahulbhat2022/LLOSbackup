# The Weavers Quarter

**"A man is what his cloth says he is. Everything else is just opinion."**

---

## Overview

The Weavers Quarter is quieter than its neighbours — the sound of looms is rhythmic and low, the dye vats bubble without drama, the tailoring rooms operate in focused near-silence. The district processes, produces, and controls every form of textile that passes through the Hearth: raw cloth and finished garments, common work-wear and the fine-stitched guild regalia that district heads wear to Council meetings, canvas sacking and arcane-threaded formal cloaks that shift colour with the wearer's mood.

It is also the district that enforces caste clothing law.

Caste stamps are permanent marks, but caste *presentation* — the garments a person wears, the symbols they display, the colours they assume — is regulated by code across Solace. The Weavers Quarter holds the licensed authority to produce caste-correct garments for the Hearth and most of the city's middle districts. Wearing a garment that misrepresents your stamp — a Marktfolk hem on a Gutterborn body, a Wall Born's gilded collar on Marktfolk shoulders — is a civil violation enforced by Wardens and worth steep fines. The Weavers Quarter writes the specifications. The Weavers Quarter certifies the goods. The Weavers Quarter is very interested in keeping this system complicated.

---

## Callum Shorn — Head of the Weavers Quarter

Callum Shorn is a thin, immaculate man who always appears to be cold despite the constant warmth of the Quarter's dye-houses. He wears clothes of precise, unshowy quality — nothing that would draw attention, everything that would pass the closest inspection. His hair is colourless and his eyes are pale grey and he has a manner of looking at people that starts at their shoes and works upward before he speaks, as if completing an assessment.

He runs the **Weavers Quarter** through the **Cloth Certification Board**, which manages licensing, production standards, and — most importantly — caste garment specifications. He personally authored the most recent revision to the Hearth's clothing codes, which added fourteen new sub-categories of prohibited styling. This made compliance harder, enforcement more frequent, and the market for his Board's certified goods more essential.

What Callum sells through the front door is compliance. What he sells through a secondary operation — conducted through three unremarkable courier services he nominally owns through intermediaries — is its opposite. The **False Hem** network produces and distributes illegally convincing caste-misrepresentation garments: Marktfolk coats cut to Wall Born specifications, Wall Born collar-work on Hearthfolk cloth, and occasionally — at extraordinary price — something that might pass a checkpoint scrutiny from a distance. He has sold to desperate Ashenfolk trying to access higher districts, to bored Wall Born playing at slumming, and to at least two people he later quietly confirmed were Embers scouts needing better disguises.

He sells to anyone. He has no politics. He has a margin.

**Voice:** Measured and slightly formal. He gives the impression of choosing each word against a longer list of acceptable alternatives. He does not argue; he corrects.

## Notable Locations

- **The Board Hall** — Where caste garment certifications are issued and code violations reviewed. The clerks are meticulous. The fines are documented. The waiting room is always full.
- **The Dye Quarter** — A dense row of vat houses where raw cloth is processed. The runoff stains the gutters in shifting colours. Ashenfolk crews work the vats on long shifts for below-district-standard wages, technically licensed as "apprentice labour."
- **Shorn & Associate Tailors** — Callum's flagship workshop, which produces the fine-quality certified garments that district heads and guild officers commission. It is excellent work and its prices reflect that.
- **The Hemline** — A nondescript alterations shop two spoke-roads back from the main avenue, which serves as the False Hem's primary drop point. The woman who runs it claims to be independent. She is not.

## Quest Hook — Something to Wear

An Ashenfolk family has been saving for two years to afford a certified mid-caste garment set for their daughter, who secured a legitimate Marktfolk work placement through a clerical error in the registration system — an error nobody has corrected because the employer needed the labour and didn't ask questions. The placement requires caste-correct dress. The certified cost is precisely out of reach. Someone in the Quarter is offering an alternative. The party enters a situation where the right answer is also the illegal one, and the legal answer is also the cruel one.
