# The Bone and Needle Quarter

**"Everyone bleeds the same. The question is who can afford to stop."**

---

## Overview

The Bone and Needle Quarter is where the Hearth's body goes when it breaks. Surgeons, physicians, bone-setters, apothecary-practitioners, and wound-closers operate from clean-fronted clinics along the Quarter's main avenue — which is, notably, cleaner than the rest of the Hearth. The gutters are swept twice daily. The building facades are whitewashed every season. The smell is antiseptic and faintly herbal. It is the only district in the Hearth where the visual presentation is calibrated to inspire confidence rather than comfort.

The Quarter provides legitimate medical care, certified to the Craft Council's health standards. It is not free. A broken bone costs what a smith earns in four days. Fever treatment costs what a tavern worker earns in a week. The Quarter has a means-tested access program that covers the most critical interventions for workers who cannot pay — funded, nominally, by a guild wellness levy. In practice the means test is designed to fail most applicants on minor procedural grounds, and the wellness levy mostly funds the Quarter's administrative overhead.

There is a second practice here, operating behind the legitimate front: **arcane body modification**. Grafts, implants, nerve-threading, bone reinforcement, limb augmentation using magical materials. Theoretically illegal without a special dispensation from the Craft Council. Practically available at significant price from three practitioners who have an understanding with the district head about what gets reported and what doesn't.

---

## Lady Moir Haste — Head of the Bone and Needle Quarter

Moir Haste is Wall Born who made the unusual choice to move down into the Hearth — passing over the more prestigious Wall Born medical institutions to build something she controlled completely. She is in her fifties, tall, with very clean hands and the particular quality of stillness that surgeons develop: an ability to move without hesitation and stop without urgency, as if the body is always exactly where it intends to be.

She runs the **Quarter Medical Authority** — which sets clinical standards, issues practice licenses, and oversees the means-testing program. Her clinic, the **Haste Clinic**, is the quarter's best-equipped and best-regarded facility. She does excellent work. Her patient outcomes are genuinely good. This is important to her — she did not come to the Hearth to perform; she came to practice medicine the way she wanted to practice it, without the hierarchical oversight of Wall Born institutions telling her what was acceptable.

What the oversight would have told her, specifically, is that the consent forms for experimental procedures must be comprehensible to the patient signing them. Moir's consent forms are comprehensive, accurate, and approximately three pages long in technical medical language. She has never been accused of deceiving a patient. She has, on two occasions, allowed a patient who could not pay for treatment to agree to participate in a follow-up study in lieu of partial payment. The follow-up study involved their recovery data being used to refine her grafting techniques. They were told this. They did not understand it. She documented that they were told.

The arcane modification work she runs through an associate's clinic two streets over, under a name that is not hers. The work is real, the results are real, the liability is not attached to her. The clientele includes Ring fighters seeking edge, Warden officers who want enhancement they cannot log, and at least one Council member she has never publicly acknowledged treating.

She is not cruel. She genuinely helps people. She has simply decided that she is best placed to determine what "help" means.

**Voice:** Calm and clinical, with the particular warmth of someone who has learned to put patients at ease. Direct about prognosis. Evasive, with professional smoothness, about cost and consent.

## Notable Locations

- **The Haste Clinic** — The district's flagship facility. Twelve treatment rooms, a small surgery theatre, a private recovery ward for paying clients, and a public ward for means-tested cases that is clean but crowded. The lobby has good light and uncomfortable chairs.
- **The Quarter Avenue** — The main medical commercial strip, where a dozen smaller clinics and specialist practitioners operate. Bone-setters, apothecary-practitioners, and wound-closers mostly. Not all of them are as skilled as they claim.
- **The Reliquary** — What Moir calls her personal research archive: a storage room in the Clinic's basement containing preserved surgical specimens, experimental records, and the results of eleven grafting procedures conducted over three years.
- **The Associate's Clinic** — The modification parlour two streets off the main avenue. Plain exterior, locked door, appointment only. The person who answers the door is not Moir. The hands that do the work sometimes are.

## Quest Hook — The Unfinished Patient

A Ring fighter came to the Associate's Clinic for reinforcement work — bone threading to take more punishment — and something went wrong. He's alive but the graft is deteriorating and he can feel it. The Clinic won't acknowledge he was ever there. The Haste Clinic won't see him without a referral and full payment upfront. He's running out of time and he knows the name of the person who did the work. What he needs is someone to use it.
