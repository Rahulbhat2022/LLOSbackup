**Vikram "Agni" Betal – The Phantom of Fire**  
Vikram "Agni" Betal is a **phantom of the Lower Wards**, a specter who dances on the edges of nobility’s nightmares. In the eyes of the Highborn, he is nothing more than a **terrorist, a mad arsonist**, a **dangerous radical who thrives on destruction**. But to the downtrodden, he is a **myth, a necessary evil, a story whispered in the dead of night**—a ghost who walks between the worlds of the living and the dead, bringing **truth wrapped in fire**.  
He is **inspired by the legend of Vikram and Betal**, the clever king and the ghostly riddler who clung to his back. But in this tale, Vikram and Betal are the same person. **Vikram is the man, the revolutionary, the tactician. Betal is the specter, the whispering voice of the oppressed, the one who demands stories of justice, the one who will not let him rest.** His mind is divided, torn between his **artist’s soul** and the **violence he knows is necessary**.  
**Who Is Vikram "Agni" Betal?**  
Vikram was once **a celebrated artist**, a sculptor whose hands crafted visions of the city’s lost history in marble, wood, and glass. His works, haunting and evocative, depicted **the suffering of the forgotten**—but suffering in art was **tolerable** to the Highborn. It was suffering in action they could not abide.  
When his **masterpiece**—a sprawling mural of the city's founding, where the Highborn were shown **not as saviors, but as conquerors**—was **burned by order of the magistrates**, something in him snapped. That night, **he set fire to the Grand Gallery**, ensuring that if his work could not be seen, neither would theirs. But unlike the nobles, he does not erase. **He replaces.**  
Every act of destruction is **artistic**, **symbolic**, and **calculated**. He does not simply burn—he **paints in flame**, sculpts in **ash and ruin**. His works appear **overnight, unexplained**, leaving behind only his mark:  
**Two of Vikram's Most Infamous "Exhibits"**  
**"The Stained Glass Massacre"**  
Vikram targeted the **Cathedral of the High Sun**, a place of worship **reserved for nobility**, its vast stained-glass windows depicting the **divine favor of the Highborn bloodlines**. One night, the glass **burst into flame**, but rather than shatter, it **melted**—the heat precise enough to **twist the saints’ faces into agonized screams**.  
The next morning, when the fire had cooled, a new **"mural"** had been revealed in the molten glass—**a vision of the Scourge**, the drowned slum, rising like a tide to consume the cathedral in fire and water. The Highborn **hastily removed the remains**, but **the image lived on in rumor, song, and hastily scrawled graffiti** across the city walls.  
**"The Burning Manuscript"**  
Target: **The Grand Archive of the Eternal Histories**  
The **Grand Archive** is the beating heart of the Highborn’s **control over history**, a massive library where only the nobility and their scholars may enter. Within its vaults lie **forbidden texts**, records of **uprisings erased**, and **laws rewritten** to suit those in power. The poor know only what the Highborn allow them to know.  
One night, **fire broke out in the Archive’s highest tower**. But instead of reducing the entire library to ash, the fire **burned in controlled patterns**, devouring only **specific books and scrolls**. When the flames subsided, the **vault doors burst open**, and thousands of pages—**half-burned, blackened, and torn**—rained down upon the city streets like dying embers.  
But these were **not random pages**.  
When pieced together, they revealed **hidden truths**—confessions of **fabricated histories**, proof of **rewritten laws**, and even **records of noble families who were once slumlords and tyrants** before the Highborn revised their lineage to appear “divinely chosen.”  
At dawn, crowds gathered in the streets, **reading aloud from the charred pages**. The Highborn tried to confiscate them, but the damage had been done.
