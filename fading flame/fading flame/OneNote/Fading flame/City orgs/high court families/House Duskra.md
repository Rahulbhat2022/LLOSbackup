**House Duskra — The Wardens of the Wall**
*"The wards hold. They have always held. They will continue to hold."*
*(Lord Emric Duskra, when asked. He did not make eye contact.)*

---

**Domain:** Ward Maintenance, City Shield Infrastructure, Arcane Structural Engineering
**Current Head:** Lord Emric Duskra
**Cultural Inspiration:** Nuclear engineers — the people who understand the reactor, surrounded by people who only care that the lights stay on

---

**Overview**
House Duskra built the magical wards that protect Solace from the outside world. More precisely: an ancestor built them, generations ago, and every Duskra since has been responsible for keeping them functional. The wards are why the city survives. They are the reason the ruins outside the walls remain outside. They are the first and last line between the population of Solace and everything the Satarum left behind.

This makes House Duskra simultaneously the most essential and the most dangerous family in the High Courts. They are the only ones who fully understand the ward architecture. They are the only ones who could take it apart. This is not a fact that is spoken aloud in polite company, but it is a fact that sits in the room whenever Duskra is present.

The family does not trade on this. They don't need to. They simply exist, and the wards continue to function, and everyone else sleeps.

---

**Lord Emric Duskra**
The current head of the family is a man in his late forties who has never entirely figured out why people attend social events. He goes because he has to. He leaves early. In the hours between arriving and leaving he can usually be found near an exit, or occasionally standing still in the middle of a room running calculations that have nothing to do with the conversation he is nominally having.

He is not unfriendly. He is simply not present in the way that social events require. His mind is elsewhere — specifically, it is usually in the ward matrix, reviewing outputs, checking tolerances, thinking about the degradation curve that he has been thinking about for the past six years.

He does not look like someone carrying a secret. He looks like someone carrying a technical problem they cannot put down.

SPEAKING NOTE: Emric answers questions slightly too literally, as if clarifying a specification rather than making conversation. He doesn't do small talk but he will talk at length about something technical if asked. If pushed on the wards, he says they are holding — and then says something slightly different the second time, as if correcting his own phrasing. *"The wards are stable. The primary systems are stable."* He doesn't notice the difference. The party might.

---

**The Bastion Mechanism**
The physical infrastructure of the wards is distributed across a network of anchor points embedded in the city's foundations and outer walls. The central control nexus — where the wards are monitored, adjusted, and repaired — is located within the Duskra estate, in a sub-level that has never been open to outside inspection.

The Bastion Mechanism is a room full of equipment that predates current understanding of ward theory by several generations. Emric and his team work with it primarily through inference — they understand what it does well enough to maintain it, but the underlying principles were documented by ancestors and some of that documentation has been lost. Maintenance, for the last several decades, has been a process of careful observation and cautious patch-work rather than principled repair.

This is not something House Duskra has disclosed.

---

**The Degradation**
The wards are not failing. They are weakening — slowly, measurably, in ways that Emric has been tracking for six years without telling anyone. The primary systems remain functional. The redundant systems are at sixty percent of their original capacity. The tertiary failsafes, which were never meant to be load-bearing, are now carrying more than they should.

Emric's current projection: if the degradation continues at its present rate, the tertiary systems fail within fifteen to twenty years. What happens after that depends on the primary and secondary systems holding — which they likely will, for a time, but without redundancy, a single unexpected stress event becomes a problem with no backup.

He has not disclosed this because he does not yet have a solution, and disclosing a problem without a solution in the High Courts is not how the Duskra family survives. He is looking for the solution first. He has been looking for six years.

---

**Relationships**
- **House Cerath:** A mutual understanding between two families who provide something essential. They don't socialize but they communicate. When the Garden Vaults expanded their water processing, Duskra assessed whether it would affect the ward anchor points in that sector. It didn't. Cerath thanked them without warmth.
- **The Sanctum:** Every High Courts family knows they depend on the wards. None of them have asked for a technical briefing in living memory. Emric finds this simultaneously convenient and infuriating.
- **House Mourne:** Emric is the only member of the High Courts who has asked Seraphine Mourne for access to the original ward construction documentation — the historical records from when the wards were first built. Some of those records were filed with Mourne notaries. Seraphine told him that the relevant vault has restricted access pending a procedural review. That conversation happened three years ago. The review has not concluded.

---

**Secret**
The wards are degrading and Emric is out of time on solving it quietly. What he hasn't told anyone — what he barely admits to himself — is that he found a reference in the original documentation to a failsafe built into the ward architecture by its original designer. A hard reset mechanism. He doesn't know where it is, what it requires, or what a reset would do to the city while it was happening. He has been looking for it for two years. The reference is in a section of documentation he only has partial access to, because House Mourne's review is still pending.
