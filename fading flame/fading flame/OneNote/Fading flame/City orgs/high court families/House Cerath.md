**House Cerath — The Providers**
*"We feed the High Courts. We have always fed the High Courts. This has never been more relevant than now."*

---

**Domain:** Agricultural Magic, Food and Water Production, Self-Sufficiency Infrastructure
**Current Heads:** Corvin and Isolde Cerath (twins, joint leadership)
**Cultural Inspiration:** Landed gentry who turned their land into a utility company

---

**Overview**
The High Courts' claim to self-sufficiency — independence from the lower city, from trade, from need — rests entirely on House Cerath. They operate the vast magical greenhouse complexes, the ether-powered water purification systems, and the arcane agricultural processes that produce food at yields no natural farm could approach. Without Cerath, the High Courts eat what the lower city grows. They have never let anyone forget this.

Most noble families control something they produce. Cerath controls something everyone consumes. They don't deal in luxuries or refinements. They deal in the baseline. And the baseline, it turns out, is more leverage than almost anything.

The food served at High Courts events is almost always a Cerath product. This includes the calorie-empty enchanted cuisine that has become fashionable among the nobility — food engineered for flavor without physical consequence, taste divorced from nourishment. House Cerath frames this as refinement. It is also an extremely effective demonstration of what their magic can do to something as fundamental as eating.

---

**Corvin and Isolde Cerath**
The twins are in their early thirties and run the family jointly with an arrangement that has never been fully explained to outsiders and has never needed to be. They do not divide responsibilities clearly — they share everything, rotate everything, and are almost never found in separate rooms during social events. Isolde tends to open conversations. Corvin tends to close them. The effect is of speaking to a single very composed mind with two mouths.

They are not warm but they are not cold either. They are interested — in problems, in people, in the mechanics of how things work. They treat social events like the greenhouse complexes: systems to understand, variables to optimize.

SPEAKING NOTE: The twins have a habit of beginning a sentence and letting the other finish it without pause — not performatively, just naturally. It unsettles people who encounter it for the first time. Use this at the table whenever the party speaks to them. Let Corvin start something and Isolde complete it without missing a beat.

---

**The Garden Vaults**
A vast complex beneath and alongside the Cerath estate — tiered greenhouse chambers, water processing halls, cultivation floors organized by magical output rather than crop type. The upper levels are visible from the High Courts if you know where to look: rows of illuminated glass panels built into the hillside, running through cycles of light at hours that don't match the sun.

The lowest levels are not open to visitors. Cerath staff only. What happens in the lower chambers is described in internal documentation as "waste processing" — the byproduct of large-scale agricultural magic at this output level. What the waste is, where it goes, and what it does when it gets there has never been disclosed. There are water channels running down from the complex that eventually reach the Strand and the lower city.

---

**Relationships**
- **House Duskra:** The two families share a mutual understanding — both provide something essential that would be catastrophic to lose. They don't socialise much but they acknowledge each other with the specific respect of two people who have read the same contract.
- **House Ashvane:** Cerath has no patience for prestige without function. They find House Ashvane's reliance on historical legacy quietly embarrassing and make no effort to hide it.
- **The Seraph:** The Seraph would very much like to understand exactly how the Garden Vaults work. Cerath is aware of this and finds it amusing.

---

**Secret**
The waste output from the Garden Vaults — the byproduct of agricultural magic at scale — drains into water channels that feed into the lower city. House Cerath has known this for decades. They have never investigated what long-term exposure to this runoff does to the people downstream, because the people downstream are not the people they produce food for. The connection between the runoff and certain chronic conditions in the Strand and below has never been formally studied. Cerath has not funded that study.
