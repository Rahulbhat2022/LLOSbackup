# Quest: The Weight of the Scales

**Quest Giver:** Master Glint _(presented through a proxy — a respectable-looking Wall Born legal clerk named Aldous Prim, who works for Glint without knowing the full picture)_ **Location:** The Committee Chambers, The Ledge, surrounding city districts **Recommended Level:** 4–6 **Tone:** Political intrigue, moral ambiguity, slow-burn betrayal

---

## The Setup

Master Glint has had a comfortable arrangement with the Impartial Judiciary Committee for years. Three of the five Readers assigned to the Debt Hearings are on his payroll — not dramatically, not with bags of coin passed in alleys, but through the slow drip of favors: good seats at events, debts quietly forgiven, family members offered better work. The rulings during Debt Hearings have, for years, landed consistently in Glint's favor. He calls it "an investment in orderly process."

The problem is **Weigher Isaura Venn.**

Isaura is a mid-ranking Weigher who was recently rotated onto the Debt Hearings panel as a replacement(appointed in by valcourt) after the previous Reader fell ill. She's meticulous, politically naive, and possessed of the particular moral certainty of someone who entered the law believing it was a system worth believing in. She's been quietly reviewing past Debt Hearing records as part of her panel onboarding — and the patterns are starting to bother her. She hasn't said anything to anyone yet. But she's started asking questions. And she's started documenting.

Glint cannot touch her directly. The Committee has just enough legitimate authority that a Reader turning up dead or harassed would invite scrutiny he doesn't want. He needs the problem solved _cleanly._ He needs her discredited, removed, or scared off — and he needs it done by people who have no visible connection to him.

Enter the party.

---

## The Hook — How the Party Gets Involved

The party is approached not by Glint himself, but by **Aldous Prim**, a soft-spoken Wall Born clerk who presents as a concerned citizen and minor legal administrator. He meets them in a respectable tea house in the Ledge.

Aldous explains — with evident discomfort, as if he hates involving outsiders — that he works as a procedural monitor for the Committee, tasked with flagging irregularities. He has a problem. A Reader named Isaura Venn has been accessing sealed case archives she has no jurisdiction over. She's been removing documents for "personal review." Some of those documents contain sensitive financial information about private citizens. He's concerned she may be building a case to sell — either to a criminal faction or a political rival — and that her access could compromise dozens of ongoing proceedings.

He needs investigators who aren't connected to the Committee — because if this goes internal, it becomes a political event, and Isaura has allies. He needs someone to follow her, find out who she's meeting with, and recover whatever documents she may have removed. He offers a modest but fair rate. He seems genuinely worried. He's not lying — he _is_ genuinely worried, because Glint told him just enough of the truth to make him a useful messenger.

---

## Act One — The Investigation Begins

The party begins surveilling Isaura. What they find, initially, looks suspicious on the surface if they're not careful:

- She is meeting privately with **an Ashenfolk woman named Brix** — a petitioner with an open case that Isaura is not assigned to. This looks like either corruption or inappropriate contact with a litigant.
- She has checked out sealed archive boxes and brought them to a private reading room. The archive logs show she has not returned two of them.
- She has made three visits to **a print house in the Ledge** — the kind that produces pamphlets.
- She has been seen speaking with a junior Committee scribe named **Perrin**, who looks nervous afterward.

None of this is what it appears to be. But the party doesn't know that yet.

**What is actually happening:**

- Brix is a petitioner whose brother was killed in a Strand factory collapse. Her case has been sitting for 16 months. Isaura took on the case unofficially because she recognized the delays were suspicious — the case should have moved months ago.
- The sealed archive boxes contain records of past Debt Hearing rulings. Isaura has found that the same three creditors — all connected to Glint — have won 94% of disputed claims over six years. She hasn't returned the boxes because she's still transcribing the data.
- The print house visit: she is pricing the cost of reproducing her findings for distribution, not yet committed to doing it.
- Perrin is nervous because Isaura asked him to quietly verify whether the ruling records have been altered. He checked. Some of them have.

---

## Act Two — The Pressure Builds

Glint, through Prim, increases urgency. He tells the party that Isaura has made contact with a known agitator (implying Embers affiliation) and that the window to recover the documents quietly is closing. He offers a bonus.

Meanwhile, the party may begin to notice cracks in the story they've been given:

**Clues the party can find if they dig:**

- _DC 13 Investigation (archive records):_ Aldous Prim has no formal role as a "procedural monitor" — that position doesn't exist in Committee records. He's listed as a financial liaison to external contractors.
- _DC 15 Persuasion or DC 12 Insight (talking to Brix):_ Brix is clearly not a criminal contact. She's a grieving woman who was shocked that anyone in the Committee bothered to help her. She mentions Isaura by name with something approaching reverence.
- _DC 14 Investigation (print house):_ The order Isaura placed was for 200 copies of a summary document — not a political pamphlet, but a formatted legal brief.
- _DC 16 Perception (surveilling Isaura directly):_ She looks exhausted, not conspiratorial. She eats lunch alone. She keeps glancing over her shoulder. She's scared of something — but she's pressing on anyway.
- _Talking to Perrin (DC 13 Persuasion, he's terrified):_ He confirms the records show signs of alteration. He won't say more. He begs them not to tell anyone he said anything.

---

## The Three Paths

By mid-quest, the party should have enough to understand that something is wrong with the job they've taken — though how much they've figured out depends on how thoroughly they investigated.

### Path A — They Figure It Out and Side with Isaura

The party confronts Isaura directly. She is guarded at first — she's already worried someone is watching her — but if the party gives her reason to trust them, she lays it out: Glint has owned the Debt Hearings for years. She has the proof. She needs time and she needs the documents to get out safely.

**What they can do:**

- Escort her and her documents to Anastasia Valcort, who has both the political standing to act on the information and the motivation to do so.
- Help Perrin disappear before Glint's people can silence him.
- Leak the documents to the Jesters, who will distribute them in ways that can't be contained — though this option has chaotic consequences the party can't fully control.
- Take the case directly to Arbiter Maren Cass in the Upper Chamber — the one Weigher who might actually do something.

**Consequences:** Glint loses his grip on the Debt Hearings. Several Committee members are quietly removed. Brix's case gets fast-tracked and she wins. Glint does not forget. His gold eyes will find the party again in a different context, under different pretenses.

---

### Path B — They Complete the Job Without Understanding It

The party recovers Isaura's documents and delivers them to Prim, who thanks them professionally and pays them. The quest closes cleanly.

**Two weeks later**, they notice the following if they pay attention to the city around them:

- Reader Isaura Venn has been transferred to a records post in the Scourge.
- Perrin has not been seen at the Committee building.
- The next Debt Hearing results in a sweep of rulings in favor of three creditors.
- Brix's petition is marked closed — reason listed as "petitioner withdrawal." Brix did not withdraw.

The party may not find out immediately what they did. But Solace has a long memory, and so does Glint.

---

### Path C — They Confront Prim / Glint Directly

If the party figures out the con and decides to go to the source, Prim crumbles almost immediately — he genuinely didn't know all of it, and learning the full picture disturbs him. He tells them who hired him. He won't go on record. He's terrified.

Getting to Glint himself is harder. He doesn't have a public office. He receives people by appointment, in a building in the Ledge that belongs on paper to a textile concern. If the party manages to get in front of him:

**Master Glint at a meeting:**

He is perfectly pleasant. He offers them tea. His gold eyes catch the light and don't let it go. He does not deny anything — he simply reframes it.

_"The Debt Hearings are chaotic things. Debtors fabricate stories. They find sympathetic clerks who fabricate evidence. Order requires that someone ensure the proceedings remain... honest. I am that someone. Your Reader friend — she means well, I'm sure. She simply doesn't understand what she's looking at."_

He offers to double their payment to walk away. If they refuse, he's gracious about it. He tells them he hopes they reconsider. He smiles the smile that's too wide.

He will not threaten them here. He will make a note. His Giltbound do not work quickly, but they work thoroughly.

---

## Master Glint — Notes for Running Him

- He never raises his voice. He never shows anger. His displeasure is expressed as mild disappointment.
- He remembers every detail — names, debts, relationships. Meeting him feels like talking to someone who already knows your bank records.
- His gold eyes are most notable when a debt is mentioned. They seem to _focus,_ the way a cat's eyes do before a pounce.
- He does not make promises he doesn't intend to keep. He does not make threats unless he intends to follow through. The fact that he hasn't threatened the party yet should feel more ominous, not less.
- If Glint's operation is meaningfully disrupted by this quest, file it under faction consequences. His **Current Influence drops by 1** and his **Party Affinity becomes 1 (hostile)**. He will not act immediately — but he will act.

---

## Isaura Venn — Notes for Running Her

- Mid-30s, Wall Born, speaks precisely and slightly too formally even in casual conversation.
- Has been in the Committee for eleven years. Joined because she believed in it. Has spent years quietly mourning that belief while refusing to let it die entirely.
- She is not a hero. She's a bureaucrat who found something she couldn't look away from. She's exhausted and frightened and still doing it anyway.
- If the party helps her: quietly grateful, practical about it, will remember the favor.
- If the party burns her: she doesn't survive the Scourge posting. Word gets back, eventually. Whether the party cares is up to them.

|Outcome|Reward|
|---|---|
|Complete job for Prim (Path B)|300 gp each, no further contact|
|Expose Glint (Path A/C)|150 gp from Anastasia Valcort, +1 Affinity with Embers, access to Committee legal resources through Isaura|

## Threads Left Open

- **Perrin** knows more than he said. If the party finds him later, he's been keeping a second set of notes — a personal record — that goes back further than Isaura's investigation.
- **Brix**, if her case is resolved, is radicalized either toward hope (if the system worked, barely) or toward the Embers (if it didn't). Her path is worth tracking.
- **Aldous Prim**, now knowing the full picture, is quietly ashamed. He's not a villain. He might be an asset, or a liability, depending on whether Glint thinks he talked.
- The three compromised Committee Readers are never named in this quest. They're still in their seats. They're still ruling. The party has not solved the problem — they've only loosened one thread.
