
# The Silkdeep

### _"Where even the trees remember the screams."_

> [!quote] Carved into a tree at the threshold, in a script no one taught _"She does not rule the forest. She reads it."_

**Location:** [[The Hollow Reach]] — Domain III **Faction:** [[The Silk Pact]] **Ruler:** [[N'yelthara, the Thread-That-Sees]] **Theme:** Spiders · Dominion · Silk · Hunger · Charm · Transformation · Worthiness

---

## Overview

The Silkdeep is the third and deepest domain of the [[The Hollow Reach|Hollow Reach]] — the part of the forest that swallowed itself. Where the [[The Bloatwood|Bloatwood]] reeks of decay and the [[The Horngrave|Horngrave]] bleeds silence, the Silkdeep offers something more unsettling: _comfort._

The trees grow too close together. Not unnaturally so — just wrong enough that you notice it after a moment, the way a face can seem familiar before you realise it belongs to something that has never been human. Their bark is pale and smooth, worn to near-ivory by generations of silk-thread abrading the grain. Webs span every gap between branches — not in the messy tangles of common spiders but in deliberate, **architectural spans of silk that hum when the wind moves through them.** A low, constant chord that sits just below hearing.

The light in the Silkdeep is always amber. It comes from **silk lanterns** — cocoons the size of a man's torso, hanging from branches, pulsing faintly with bioluminescence. Nothing casts a shadow here. Everything is lit from every direction at once.

The floor is carpeted in dry white silk — brittle, crackling underfoot. Beneath it, pressed flat and preserved, are the hollow shapes of things that once walked here: boots, antlers, the occasional skull that was never retrieved. The silk is not a trap. **The silk is a record.**

## The Domain

### The Weblair — N'yelthara's Seat

At the absolute centre of the Silkdeep stands the **Firstspun** — a dead colossus whose trunk is four metres across, its bark completely hidden beneath centuries of layered silk. It does not fall because [[N'yelthara, the Thread-That-Sees|N'yelthara's]] web holds it.

Her weblair occupies the entire upper canopy of the Firstspun — a three-dimensional palace of silk and signal, spanning **thirty metres across and twelve metres deep.** It is not a throne room. It is not a nest. It is a sensory organ. Every major web in the Silkdeep connects to a thread that connects to here. N'yelthara sits at the centre of it and feels the whole domain like a heartbeat.

Approaching the Firstspun uninvited takes ten minutes of careful travel through progressively thicker webbing. The silk near the trunk is no longer brittle — it is fresh, strong, and sticky enough to hold a grown man. **Dozens of wrapped shapes hang from the lower branches** in various states of desiccation. Some are animals. Some are not.

---

### The Thread-Archive

The Silkdeep's most disorienting feature — half a kilometre square, where every web has been left untouched since it was first spun. Layers upon layers of silk accumulate across decades, forming **opaque white walls** between the trees that turn the space into a labyrinth of narrow corridors.

These corridors are the Silkdeep's record of judgement. Preserved upright within the silk — wrapped in place, visible in outline through the outermost membrane — are **people.** The ones who failed. Not who failed to pay, or who failed to deliver. Who failed _her test_ — who lied when she was watching, who broke under pressure when she needed to see if they would, who showed her something small and irredeemable about what they were. N'yelthara does not punish weakness. She simply files it. The Archive is where the unworthy go.

> [!note] The Archive as Evaluation Every figure in the Archive was given a chance first. That is the only thing N'yelthara insists on — everyone gets a chance. What she does with you after depends entirely on what you showed her. The Spiderlings passed. The Monstrous tried to fake it. The Archive is everyone else.

Some of the preserved figures are still alive — N'yelthara's silk slows metabolic processes dramatically. Whether she intends to revisit these judgements, or whether being filed here _is_ the judgement, is not something she has clarified. [[Verath of the Eight Eyes]] maintains the Archive and knows the name, circumstance, and test-outcome of every figure within it.

## The Silk Pact — Faction

> [!info] Faction at a Glance **Type:** Meritocracy of the proven / cult of worthiness **Numbers:** ~80 Spiderlings, 3 High Weavers, 1 Matron **Creature Types:** Cursed Ones (Drow sub-type), Giant Spiders, Chimeras (the Monstrous) **Material Weakness:** Silver (Drow); None standard (Spider-forms) **Oils:** Cursed Oil (standard Drow); Superior Insectoid Oil (Spider-fused); Superior Chimera Oil (the Monstrous) **Disposition:** Isolationist, predatory, diplomatically capable when amused **City Presence:** Hidden — at least two agents operating in [[Haven|Haven's]] black markets

The Silk Pact is not a military force. It is **a hierarchy of the proven.** Every member has been tested by N'yelthara — not in a ritual sense, but in the way she tests everything: she watched, she waited, she put pressure on the thread and observed whether it held. Those who passed became Spiderlings. Those who tried to deceive her became the Monstrous. Those who simply failed became entries in the Thread-Archive.

N'yelthara's currency is not information, coin, or leverage. It is **worthiness** — a quality she considers herself the only reliable judge of. She is not arbitrary about this. She has clear criteria, developed over three centuries of observation. She simply does not share what they are, because telling someone what you're testing for defeats the test.

**All members of the Silk Pact are male.** N'yelthara is the only woman in the Silkdeep — by design, and by preference. She has never explained why. The Spiderlings do not ask.

### How the Silkdeep Grows

Lost men from [[Haven]] find their way into the Hollow Reach. Sometimes they are guided by circumstance. Sometimes they simply walk in the wrong direction long enough. The Silkdeep notices them before they notice the Silkdeep.

N'yelthara does not intervene immediately. She watches first — through the web, through the silk-sense, through the way a man moves when he thinks no one is looking. She is reading what he is made of. Most men, watched this way for a few hours, reveal themselves entirely. She has been doing this for three centuries. She is very fast at it now.

For the ones that interest her, she begins the test. It is never announced. It might be isolation — the forest seems to grow darker, the amber light withdrawing, and she watches what he does with fear. It might be the Drift — the spores loosening something in him, and she watches what comes up when the surface control dissolves. It might be Lissiveth appearing, friendly and ordinary, and she watches how the man treats someone he perceives as beneath him.

**Most men fail.** Not dramatically. Just quietly — they show her something small and true that tells her they are not what she is looking for. She releases them at the boundary. They do not remember the interior of the Silkdeep clearly. They remember amber light and a sense of having been briefly somewhere else.

**Some men pass.** She makes the offer. The First Weaving. Transformation into something more than they were, in exchange for belonging to something larger than themselves. Most accept. The ones who accept genuinely — who pass because they are genuinely worth passing, not because they performed well — become the Spiderlings.

**A rare few try to deceive her.** She knows immediately. These become the Monstrous.

### The Three Outcomes

|Path|What Happens|Result|
|---|---|---|
|**Worthy**|Man is tested — by the Drift, by isolation, by N'yelthara's quiet observation — and holds. Shows something real under pressure.|She makes the offer. He becomes a Spiderling.|
|**Ordinary**|Man is tested and comes up empty — not deceptive, just unremarkable. Nothing worth transforming, nothing worth filing.|Escorted back to the forest boundary. Released. He will not remember how he got there.|
|**Deceitful**|Man performs under the test — pretends a quality he doesn't have, tries to manipulate, attempts to con the Silkdeep's ruler. She knows immediately.|The Second Weaving. Becomes one of [[The Monstrous]], and goes into the Archive.|

### Faction Goals

- **Maintain the Silkdeep as inviolate** — no outsider presence, no rival domain's influence, no city encroachment
- **Expand N'yelthara's intelligence network into [[Haven]]** — she has threads in the Hollow Reach; she wants threads in every organ of power in the city
- **Acquire Source-attuned materials** — N'yelthara believes the Source strings can be physically woven like silk, and has been directing the Pact to collect components for decades
- **Keep the other two domains apart** — feed [[Mother Muckrot]] gossip, leave the [[The Antlered Sorrow|Antlered Sorrow's]] borders undisturbed
- **Avoid [[The Hollow Crown]] at all costs** — N'yelthara cannot read it, and what she cannot read she fears

## Members

### Drow Spiderlings

The Drow of the Silkdeep are made, not born. The **First Weaving** transforms a man over a single night: cocooned in fresh silk treated with N'yelthara's venom, brood-spider threads woven through his nervous system. Those who survive emerge changed.

**Physical changes after the First Weaving:**

- Skin takes a blue-grey cast
- Eyes shift to solid amber or silver
- Partial chitin exoskeleton develops — forearms, shoulders, neck — growing thicker with devotion and age
- Some develop additional eyes (always in pairs, never more than four)
- A rare few develop a vestigial spinnerette — non-functional alone, but responsive to N'yelthara's web

Spiderlings operate in **threes** — one Silk (tracker/assassin), one Thread (negotiator/intelligence), one Fang (combat anchor). Operating alone is considered a social failure. Breaking formation in the field is nearly as bad.

### The Monstrous

Men who defied N'yelthara and were given the Second Weaving. The transformation is not careful. It is not kind. It is not designed to preserve the man inside.

What emerges is a **humanoid spider-form** — the right height, the right number of limbs if you count generously, but nothing else recognisably human. Extra legs burst through the torso. The face collapses inward. The voice is gone, replaced by a clicking of mandibles. They retain just enough intelligence to follow simple orders and enough instinct to hunt effectively. They are used as guards near the Firstspun and as patrol creatures in the Thread-Archive's deepest corridors.

They cannot leave the Silkdeep. N'yelthara has never been sure if this is by design or if the transformation simply makes them refuse to go.

### The High Weavers

The three oldest Spiderlings. Each has undergone the First Weaving multiple times, each subsequent transformation pushing them further from mortal. They are utterly devoted to N'yelthara — not from charm, but from decades of genuine love for the thing they have become.

---

#### Verath of the Eight Eyes

_Master of the Thread-Archive. Intelligence coordinator._

Verath's face has been replaced almost entirely by the arrangement: **eight silver eyes in two rows**, no nose, mouth sewn shut with silk thread. He communicates through vibration patterns tapped onto surfaces, or translated by an attending Spiderling.

He knows the location of every secret in the Thread-Archive. He navigates it blindfolded — which is relevant, because he keeps his eyes closed except when reading threads. He was the first man N'yelthara transformed after taking the Silkdeep. He does not remember what he looked like before, and does not want to.

**Combat:** Does not fight. If cornered:

- _Silk Spray_ — Blinding, 4m cone, Agility DC 6 or blinded 2 rounds
- _Distress Vibration_ — Summons every spider in the Silkdeep within 60 seconds

## N'yelthara, the Thread-That-Sees

> [!quote] _"She does not have subjects. She has debts, and loves, and curiosities, and meals — and the distances between these categories are not always as wide as one might hope."_

The Spider Queen. The only woman in the Silkdeep. The reason for all of it.

---

### Appearance

N'yelthara's lower body is that of a spider — not metaphorically, not suggestively, but **accurately and completely.** Eight legs, each longer and more articulated than a horse's, covered in fine black silk-hair. Her abdomen is the size of a small cart, glossy black with deep violet iridescence, and continuously produces silk — thin threads that trail behind her like a veil wherever she moves.

Her upper body is that of a woman, and has been for so long she has forgotten what she was before. Her skin is the blue-white of fresh silk. Her face is beautiful in the way that a very still room is beautiful — nothing to find fault with, nothing that warms you. Her eyes are **six**, arranged symmetrically, each a different shade of amber or gold. She wears a veil not out of modesty but because her face, seen fully and directly, produces in most mortals a deep, wordless dread she finds socially inconvenient.

She is always suspended. She does not touch the ground if she can avoid it. In her weblair she moves through the structure like a language — fluid, purposeful, and completely at home in three dimensions in a way that makes watching her feel like watching something that understands the world differently than you do.

---

### Personality & Motivation

N'yelthara is not cruel. Cruelty implies caring whether the subject suffers. What she is, more precisely, is **exacting** — she has spent three centuries developing an eye for what people are made of, and she has very little patience for pretence.

She does not want payment. She does not want information. She does not want obedience purchased by fear. What she wants, in every interaction she has ever had, is the same thing: **to know if you are worth knowing.** This is not vanity. It is not a game. She has genuine criteria — developed through three hundred years of watching men under pressure — and she applies them with the same rigour every time. She simply never tells anyone what they are, because a test you know about is not a test.

The Spiderlings passed. She is genuinely proud of them, in the way a craftsman is proud of well-made work. The Monstrous attempted to deceive her — they performed worthiness rather than possessing it, and she knew immediately, and filed them accordingly. The Thread-Archive is everyone who came to the Silkdeep and showed her something she could not use.

She does not punish failure. She catalogues it. There is a difference, and she considers the distinction important.

Her relationship to the Silk Pact is that of a maker to her finest work. She built these men from what they already were — the transformation only brings out what was latent. She is territorial about them, attentive to them, and will dismantle anything that threatens them without raising her voice or changing her expression.

What she does to those who try to fake it is not punishment. It is simply accurate filing. She **puts them where they belong.**

### Running N'yelthara in Combat

N'yelthara does not engage casually. The last time she did so in earnest was approximately sixty years ago, and the evidence is still hanging in the Thread-Archive.

**Phase 1 — Open:** She fights from her weblair, using Thread Pull to drag enemies into range, Web Torrent to control space, and her legs to punish anyone who gets close. She speaks calmly throughout. She is curious about the party. She will ask questions between attacks.

**Phase 2 — Below half deep wounds:** She retreats into the Firstspun's web structure and fights through the domain itself — pulling enemies into silk corridors, using The Monstrous as a screen, and collapsing web walls to separate the party. She stops talking.

**Surrender:** Before the final deep wound, N'yelthara will offer terms. What she offers will be exactly what the party needs most. She has been listening to the web the entire fight.

> [!warning] She Does Not Die Easily N'yelthara is three hundred years old. She has survived everything the Hollow Reach has thrown at her. If retreat is viable, she takes it. Killing her requires either trapping her completely or removing her ability to produce silk — which she will resist with every resource available.

## Adventure Hooks

### The Offer

The party wakes to find a single silk thread tied around one member's wrist — placed there somewhere between sleep and waking. The thread leads toward the Hollow Reach. N'yelthara has decided they are worth watching. This is not yet an invitation. It is the beginning of a test she has not described. **Refusing to follow the thread is, itself, data.**

### The Filed

Someone the party knows has been released from the Silkdeep — wandered back to the city three days ago, remembers almost nothing, and has been quietly wrong ever since. Not dangerous. Not transformed. Just hollowed out in some way they can't articulate, as if something was assessed and found lacking. They don't know what happened. The party, if they investigate, will eventually reach N'yelthara. She will tell them plainly: he was tested. He failed. She let him go. She always lets the ones who fail go. She will seem to think this makes her generous.

### The Standard

A Spiderling has been operating in the city and has done something N'yelthara considers beneath her standard — not disobedience, but a failure of quality. He panicked. He acted small. She wants it corrected, but not by her own hand — having the Silk Pact police itself would undermine the test. She approaches the party because she has been watching them and thinks they might be capable of it. This is itself the test.

### The First Weaving

A city contact — merchant, informant, friend — vanished weeks ago. They entered the Hollow Reach and have not returned. Investigation reveals they are alive, in the Silkdeep, and have accepted the First Weaving. They passed her test. They have no interest in being rescued. The party must decide what that means, and whether the person they knew is still in there — or whether what's in there now is an improvement.

### Source Silk

N'yelthara has partially succeeded at something she has been attempting for decades: a length of silk woven from physical Source strings, humming faintly, that leaves temporary spellsight in anyone who touches it. She wants to test it. She wants a willing, Source-attuned subject — not purchased or coerced, but one who comes _knowing what they're agreeing to_ and agrees anyway. Finding someone with that combination of knowledge and willingness is its own test of whether the party can be trusted with a genuinely delicate task.
