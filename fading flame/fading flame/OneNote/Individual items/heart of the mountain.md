Deep beneath the **Furnace District**, the ancient forge of **Hephaestus’s Smith** lies dormant. The dwarves who once tended it were annihilated in a collapse decades ago—but the Iron Maiden has found evidence that the forge’s power source, **the Heart of the Mountain**, still pulses below.


The song: 
echoing through the cave is dwarves singing hymns of the forgesong. 
“Pour the ore… steady now…”  
“Strike true, lads, the Heart watches…”  
“Hephaestus, bless the flame…”


**Concept:**  
When the forge collapsed, Hephaestus’s divine spark fused with molten metal, trapped the dying dwarves’ souls within, and created a sentient _forging consciousness_.  
It’s not malicious—it’s _obsessive_.  
It forges endlessly, using any living being it finds as “materials.”

It doesn’t erase memory—it _reworks it_, reforging the mind like metal. Victims believe they’ve _always_ been part of the forge.


Traps:
The environment itself attacks. Forges ignite, anvils tremble, chains whip out like serpents. The Anvil’s Ghost _inhabits_ the entire forge.  
Destroying it means destroying the forge itself.


### **How It Works**

The entity — the **Anvil’s Ghost** — doesn’t eat memories. It _rewrites them._  
Anyone charmed by the song begins to **forget the surface world** and **remember** themselves as _workers_ in Hephaestus’s forge.

- The song subtly alters reality for the charmed PC.
    
- They remember a _shared history that never existed_ — the party as fellow smiths.
    
- Time becomes distorted — hours pass like minutes, or days vanish in labor.
    
- The player may find “new memories” of having worked the forge for years.
    
## **When Only One PC Becomes Charmed**

Here’s how to run it _creepily and fairly_:

1. **Isolate the Effect:**
    
    - Only one PC hears the forgesong differently.
        
    - Describe it as _beautiful_, like hearing harmony in the sound of metal.
        
    - The others just hear noise and rhythmic clanging.
        
2. **Subtle Behavioral Shift:**
    
    - That PC begins to help the machines automatically — stoking the furnace, moving molten metal, forging weapons.
        
    - When questioned, they respond naturally:
        
        > “What? I’ve always worked here. You’re slowing the shift.”
        
3. **Reality Drift:**
    
    - Other PCs notice changes:
        
        - New tools appear that no one remembers bringing.
            
        - Their charmed ally’s clothes are soot-stained and torn.
            
        - There’s a _finished blade_ on the anvil that no one saw being forged.
            
4. **Time Skip Event if all get charmed:**
    
    - The party rests — or blinks — and when they “wake,” the forge has progressed.
        
        - A weapon half-forged now gleams complete.
            
        - A clock they brought reads _three days later._
            
        - The charmed PC insists only an hour passed.
> “You remember this hammer. You made it, didn’t you? That’s your mark right there on the haft…”


## **The Core Twist**

The forgesong is **not an external sound but memories inside the parties minds**

The “song” the party hears is the _echo_ of a psychic waveform imprinted on reality by the dwarves’ collective devotion and deaths.  
When the forge collapsed, their hymns were imprinted into the Heart of the Mountain crystal like grooves in a record.  
Now, instead of sound, it plays back _thoughts._ 

Everyone interprets it as song because their mind translates the psychic rhythm into what it understands — auditory form.

When the players plug their ears, they aren’t blocking the song — they’re **isolating themselves**, making their minds _more vulnerable_ to its psychic manipulation.  
The “relief” is actually the moment they **stop resisting consciously** and let the pattern rewrite their thoughts directly.
"you havent locked me out, you locked yourself in with me"
